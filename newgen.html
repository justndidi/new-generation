Alternative . Art-soul

Alté

Nigeria's alternative movement - blending R&B, afrobeats and hip-hop with art-school energy and unapologetic weirdness. Born in lagos, felt worldwide.

EST. 2015

Alté (short for 'alternative') is the sound of Nigerian youth rejecting the mainstream of their own terms. Cruel Santino, Odunsi(The Engine), Santi, Lady Donli built the scene from lekki house parties and self-released SoundCloud tapes. It is genre-less by design -- one track might be lo-fi R&B, the next a psychedelic afrobeats experiment. The aesthetic is thrift-shop meets lagos island.

KEY ARTISTS

Cruel Santino Odunsi (The Engine) Santi Lady Donli Tay Iwar
. 41.2k monthly listeners
STREET . HEAVY BASS

Naija Drill

Lagos street energy over heavy UK drill production. Raw pidgin bars, 808 sub-bass, and storytelling from the trenches. The underground scene the blogs missed.

EST. 2019

Taking the dark drill template from Chicago and London and running it through Lagos, Naija Drill is street-first and unapologetic. Artists like Portable, Shallipopi, And Bella Shmurda blew up from the streets with zero label backing.
The lyrics are in Yoruba, Pidgin, and slang that only makes sense if you were there. Percussion hits harder here.

KEY ARTISTS

Portable Shallipopi Bella Shmurda Mohbad Lil kesh
. 28.7k monthly listeners
EXPERIMENTAL . PAN-GENRE

Afrofusion

Where afrobeats stops following rules. Amapiano log drums, trap hi-hats, R&B melodies, and vocals runs that bend every genre they touch.

EST. 2017

Afrofusion is the sound of a generation that grew up on everything at once. Artists like Victony, FAVE, Arya starr and Ruger pull from dancehall, Afropop, R&B, and pop without asking for permission. The production is immaculate -- Nigerian engineers are now some of the most sought-after on the continent. This is where the next wave is building.

KEY ARTISTS

Victony FAVE Ayra Starr Ruger Zinolessky
. 63.4k monthly listeners
AGEGE . PIDGIN . RAW

Street-Hop

Agege percussion, pidgin punch lines, and melodies that hit like the heat. Built in the parts of lagos the blogs don't write about. Unti now.

EST. 2018

Street-Hop is the sound of Mushin, Agege, Bariga -- the working-class heart of lagos. Artists like Seyi Vibez built massive followings without a single press feature. The rhythm is distinctly Yoruba, the delivery is conversational, and the reliability is total. When Seyi Vibez dropped his first tape, it spread entirely through WhatsaApp.

KEY ARTISTS

Seyi Vibez Zinoleesky Naira Marley Young Duu Zlatan
. 31.5k monthly listeners
BEDROOM POP . MELODIC

Afro-R&B

Intimate, lo-fi, and deeply personal. Afrobeats rhythm section meets bedroom R&B production. The emotional underbelly of the Nigerian new-gen scene.

EST. 2016

Where the party ends and the feeling stays. Artists like Tay Iwar, Boj, and Oxlade make music that lives 2am -- confessional lyrics, lush production, melodies that linger for days. It is the R&B tradition reimagined through a lagos lens, without trying to sound American. The vulnerability is intentional.

KEY ARTISTS

Tay Iwar Boj Oxlade Adekunle Gold Amaarae
. 19.8k monthly listeners
LOG DRUMS . AFROBEATS

Amapiano Lagos

South Africa's log drums arrived in Lagos and never left. Nigerian producers fused them with afrobeats bounce and Yoruba call-and-response. A genre born from a collab.

EST. 2021

When amapiano hit Nigeria, producers like Young Jonn, Bnxn, and Kdave didn't copy it -- they cracked it open and put lagos inside. The log drum is still there but the rhythm is different, the energy is rowdier, and the vocals carry pidgin and Yoruba. Amapiano Lagos lives in the clubs but the underground producers are taking it somewhere much stranger.

KEY ARTISTS

Young Jonn Bnxn Kdave Prettyboy DO Spyro
. 47.3k monthly listeners

003 / What we are

Nigeria's next
wave deserves
a platform.

The Nigerian music industry has been telling the same story for years — afrobeats, the big names, the mainstream. But the real energy has always come from below. From bedroom producers in Surulere, from street artists in Mushin, from alté kids in Lekki who never asked for permission to be different.

NewGen is for that energy. We write about the genres that blogs haven't named yet. We spotlight the artists before the labels come knocking. We believe Nigerian music is not one sound — it is hundreds of sounds, and most of them haven't been heard outside a WhatsApp forward or a small Bandcamp page.

If you are a new-gen Nigerian musician making something real, this is where you belong. No follower count minimum. No label required. Just your music.
